Research problem

What happens between commitment and practice?

Learner-centredness is prominent in policy and institutional discourse. The research asks how those commitments are interpreted, translated and made meaningful through institutional processes.

What happens between an institutional commitment to learner-centredness and its eventual experience by learners?

Central research question

How is learner-centredness institutionally enacted?

1. How is learner-centredness constructed within national policy and sectoral discourse?

2. How is learner-centredness interpreted and operationalised within selected institutional case sites?

3. How do institutional actors describe the governance, interpretive and relational processes through which it is enacted?

4. What recurring patterns, tensions, mechanisms and institutional conditions can be identified?

Current analytical scaffold

Following the enactment pathway

Commitment

Policy commitment → interpretation → translation

Enactment

Institutional decision or process → enacted practice

Recursion

Learner experience → feedback and re-enactment

The pathway is iterative. It provides a way of asking where interpretation, discretion and institutional processes shape what learner-centredness becomes.

Analytical distinction

Conditions and mechanisms

Condition

A circumstance that shapes what becomes possible, such as regulation, resources, governance or organisational history.

Mechanism

The process through which a condition is interpreted and translated into institutional action.

The distinction is provisional and will be tested through the documentary and later empirical work.

Ecological perspective

Enactment occurs across interacting contexts

Macro / external

National policy, regulation, funding, quality frameworks and sector narratives.

Meso / institutional

Governance, strategy, resources, structures, leadership, culture and institutional history.

Micro / local

Departments, teams, professional roles, everyday practices and staff–learner relationships.

Inter-organisational influences, including sector bodies, accreditors, partnerships and networks, cut across these contexts. The ecological perspective is used to examine interaction rather than to assign each factor to a fixed level.
