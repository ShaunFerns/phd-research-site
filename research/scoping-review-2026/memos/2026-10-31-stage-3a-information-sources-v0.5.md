# Stage 3A — Information sources and database selection (working v0.5)

**Date:** 31 October 2026  
**Status:** Provisional methodological recommendation; database set not yet frozen.  
**Review:** Institutional Enactment of Learner-Centredness in Higher Education (2015–2026).

## Purpose

Select proportionate information sources capable of locating international scholarly research on how learner-centredness is interpreted and enacted in higher education organisations. The formal scoping review evidence search is distinct from the later Irish institutional-document corpus.

## Core candidate databases

1. **Scopus:** interdisciplinary higher education, organisational and governance literature.
2. **Web of Science Core Collection:** complementary interdisciplinary citation-index coverage.
3. **ERIC:** specialist education, curriculum, institutional and policy research.
4. **ProQuest Dissertations & Theses:** doctoral studies potentially providing detailed institutional case research and practice-based/led scholarship; retain provisionally pending access and added-value review.

**Further candidate:** Education Source (EBSCO) or ProQuest Education Database, subject to library access and testing. A 2025 journal-coverage study found ERIC alone insufficient among specialist education databases. Test marginal relevance and uniqueness before deciding to add a fifth source.

## Search process

Follow the JBI three-step principle: exploratory searching to identify seed studies/terminology/index terms; translated systematic searches across agreed databases; supplementary citation searching and appropriately bounded additional discovery. Log platforms, dates, limits, complete query translations and retrieved records.

## Supplementary sources

Targeted backward/forward citation searching on eligible studies; documented checking of relevant existing syntheses. Consider a bounded Google Scholar check. Do not automatically search all higher education institutional websites worldwide: that would blur the review with the separate Irish institutional-document corpus. Relevant research reports may be eligible if the later publication-type decision permits them and a defensible retrieval method is specified.

## Decisions still open

- Verify TU Dublin access to the proposed specialist education source and dissertation database.
- Define treatment of books and chapters, doctoral theses and published research reviews.
- Define whether and how institutional research reports form part of the scholarly corpus.
- Confirm practical English-language restriction and justify international implications.
- Test search sensitivity against a small verified set of independently identified, demonstrably eligible studies; test database contributions and deduplication load before freezing sources.
- Treat publication eligibility end date (30 September 2026) separately from actual search execution dates.

## Methodological judgement

Four core candidates are reasonable but not automatically sufficient. Database selection should be justified by topical and methodological coverage, retrieval feasibility and demonstrable additional relevant evidence; neither minimal database count nor maximum volume is an end in itself.

## Reflective note

The design should capture scholarship crossing educational policy, organisational change, programme design and practice-based inquiry without demanding an infeasible worldwide institutional website search. A specialist education database might contribute relevant unique studies; its value should be tested rather than assumed.

## Publication-type refinement — 31 October 2026 (provisional)

The preferred boundary is to include journal research articles and completed doctoral theses (including professional doctorates), without excluding practice-based/led doctoral research. Master's dissertations would be excluded as a proportionate source-type boundary, not because their research is necessarily inferior. Scholarly books and edited chapters would ordinarily support the wider doctoral literature review rather than form part of the formal scoping review; this sacrifices some original research and must be acknowledged. Existing research reviews would primarily support contextualisation and citation searching, with a final decision on eligibility to follow. Reports of the same investigation (e.g. a thesis and associated article) should be linked as reports of one study rather than counted as independent studies. These positions remain provisional until publication-type criteria are finalised.

## Source overlap and study-level linkage — 31 October 2026

Prior bibliographic exploration indicated recurring overlap between ProQuest doctoral theses and articles or reports indexed elsewhere. This observation is a reason to test ProQuest's *unique evidential contribution* at the next database pilot, not proof that doctoral theses add no value. Distinguish exact duplicate bibliographic records from different reports of one research investigation. Retain linked records with publication-level identifiers and a shared study/project identifier where substantiated; do not automatically collapse a thesis and article into one bibliographic record or count them as independent investigations. Chart additional institutional context from a thesis where relevant, while avoiding double counting. The new review's eligibility and reporting will be based on its prospectively documented search and assessment procedures.
