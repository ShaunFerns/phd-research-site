Research problem

Widely invoked, unevenly enacted

Learner-centredness is prominent in policy and institutional discourse, but its practical meaning is interpreted, negotiated and operationalised within particular institutional settings.

How does a policy commitment become an institutional practice?

Research objective

Examine institutional enactment

To investigate how learner-centredness is constructed in Irish higher-education policy and how those commitments are interpreted, translated and enacted within institutional settings.

The study connects national policy, public institutional documentation, selected institutional cases and practice-based representation.

Central research question

How is learner-centredness institutionally enacted?

1. How is learner-centredness constructed within national policy and sectoral discourse?

2. How is learner-centredness interpreted and operationalised within selected institutional case sites?

3. How do institutional actors describe the governance, interpretive and relational processes through which it is enacted?

4. What recurring cross-case patterns, tensions, mechanisms and institutional conditions can be identified?

Research design

Three connected strands

01

National policy and sectoral documentary analysis, including public institutional mapping across Irish HEIs

02

Comparative qualitative case study of selected institutional sites and participant accounts

03

Practice-based analytic documentary artefact as a mode of representational inquiry

Conceptual framing

Policy enactment rather than simple implementation

Implementation asks

Was the policy put into practice?

Enactment asks

How was it interpreted, translated, negotiated and made meaningful in context?

This framing keeps attention on the relationship between policy texts, governance processes, professional roles, institutional conditions and academic practice.

Progress to date

Initial research architecture established

Research focus: refined from a broad rhetoric–reality problem to institutional enactment of learner-centredness.

Design: clarified the relationship between national/sectoral documentary analysis and selected institutional case sites.

Theory: policy enactment established as the primary lens, with complementary attention to institutional context and sensemaking.

Methodology: practice-based qualitative design developed, with the documentary artefact positioned as part of inquiry rather than simply dissemination.

Researcher position: insider-practitioner positionality, role boundaries, reflexivity and institutional sensitivity identified as core design considerations.

Current research activity

Building the evidence and methodological foundation

Literature

Developing the literature architecture around learner-centredness, policy enactment, institutional context and higher-education practice.

Documents

Developing the national and sectoral documentary-analysis approach and the logic for public institutional mapping.

Method

Refining case-study boundaries, ethics, interview logic, reflexive practice and the role of the documentary artefact.

Contribution and impact

What the study is intended to add

Empirical: a grounded account of how learner-centred commitments are enacted within institutional settings.

Conceptual: a clearer account of the mechanisms, relationships and institutional conditions shaping enactment.

Methodological: integration of documentary analysis, institutional case study and practice-based representation.

Practical: a framework that may help institutions examine the relationship between stated learner-centred commitments and the structures through which those commitments are made real.
