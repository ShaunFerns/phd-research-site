# Scoping review methodological memo — 31 October 2026

**Review:** Institutional Enactment of Learner-Centredness in Higher Education (2015–2026)  
**Document:** Stage 1 decision memo, v0.3  
**Status:** Working methodological decision; PCC and detailed eligibility criteria to follow.

## Decision: institutional relationship without a formal policy prerequisite

The review will examine institutionally situated interpretation and enactment of learner-centredness. Inclusion will **not require an identifiable formal institutional policy, strategy or stated commitment**. Relevant research may instead investigate how organisational conditions, institutional cultures, governance, curriculum structures, programme arrangements, academic decision-making, professional practices or mechanisms for learner participation shape the enactment of learner-centred educational approaches.

The necessary boundary is a **substantive institutional relationship**. A study of an individual teaching intervention, learner outcome or classroom technique will not qualify solely because it was conducted within a university. Its analysis must contribute evidence on an institutional, faculty/school, programme or organisational relationship relevant to learner-centredness. This boundary will be operationalised and tested during PCC development and screening calibration.

## Rationale

A requirement to document a formal policy commitment would privilege explicit policy texts and formal implementation accounts. It could exclude research showing the influence of organisational arrangements, dispersed decision-making and institutional culture where no single policy text is identified. These are relevant to the doctoral investigation of interpretation, negotiation and enactment.

At the same time, permitting every learner-centred teaching intervention would make the review unmanageably broad and would weaken its contribution to the PhD. The criterion therefore concerns the *substance of the institutional relationship investigated*, not the administrative level at which data were collected.

## Working review architecture

- **Purpose:** Map research investigating institutional enactment of learner-centredness and the reported processes, actors and organisational conditions associated with it.
- **Main question (working):** How has the institutional enactment of learner-centredness been investigated in higher education research published between January 2015 and September 2026?
- **Analytical questions:** (1) What processes and institutional arrangements are reported in interpretation and translation into educational practice? (2) What organisational conditions, actors and opportunities for learner participation are identified as influencing these processes?
- **Conceptual history and wider theoretical debate:** Addressed principally within the doctoral literature review. Meanings of learner-centredness in included studies will still be charted as relevant contextual information.
- **Geographical coverage:** International, subject to formal PCC review.
- **Publication dates:** 1 January 2015–30 September 2026 (working). The revised 2015 ESG, particularly Standard 1.3, provides an Irish/EHEA policy-relevant rationale, though the international applicability of this temporal boundary requires explicit justification.
- **Conduct:** A proportionate, transparent JBI-informed approach. The doctoral researcher expects to undertake the full review, with independent screening calibration involving the principal supervisor. Departures from recommendations for duplicate screening/charting and corresponding limitations will be described accurately.

## Reflective note

The institutional boundary should reflect how enactment may be evidenced in the research, not demand that authors identify a formal policy before their findings become relevant. This broadens the kinds of institutional processes that can be examined while retaining a defensible exclusion of studies limited to routine classroom interventions. The next task is to translate this working distinction into a PCC framework and test it on ambiguous examples before final eligibility rules are adopted.
