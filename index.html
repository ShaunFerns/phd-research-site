PhD Research Overview

From Policy Commitment to Institutional Enactment

Learner-centredness in Irish higher education through literature synthesis, policy and institutional documentary analysis, comparative qualitative case study and a developing practice-based strand.

Open presentation Read overview

Research focus

This PhD examines how learner-centredness is institutionally enacted in Irish higher education. The central concern is what happens between an institutional commitment to learner-centredness and its eventual expression in policy, governance, organisational processes and practice.

Current phase · November 2026

The study is in the first year of a part-time PhD. The immediate work is deliberately preparatory: establishing the evidence base, refining the problem, testing analytical ideas and building a defensible methodological pathway before participant-facing case-study research begins.

Scoping review

Systematic mapping of research on learner-/student-centredness in higher education, guided by JBI and reported using PRISMA-ScR.

Irish HEI document corpus

Collection of public national, sectoral and institutional documents across the Irish higher education landscape, including institutional transition and predecessor contexts.

Methodological development

Ongoing work on policy enactment, case-study design, reflexivity, ethics and the role that documentary practice may eventually play in the research.

Central research question

How is learner-centredness institutionally enacted in Irish higher education, and how do institutional processes align, translate or mediate national policy commitments?

Sub-questions

  1. How is learner-centredness constructed within national higher education policy and sectoral discourse in Ireland?
  2. How is learner-centredness interpreted and operationalised within selected institutional case sites?
  3. How do institutional actors describe the governance, interpretive and relational processes through which learner-centredness is enacted?
  4. What recurring cross-case patterns and tensions can be identified in the institutional enactment of learner-centredness?
  5. What enactment mechanisms and institutional conditions can be abstracted from the selected case sites?

Scoping review progress

The formal search covers 1 January 2015 to 30 September 2026 and uses ERIC, Scopus, Web of Science Core Collection and ProQuest Dissertations & Theses. Journal articles, substantive reports and master's and doctoral theses are retained for eligibility assessment.

Irish higher education document corpus

A parallel documentary workstream is assembling public material across the full period of interest rather than sampling institutions only at the point of their current organisational form. Particular attention is being paid to the creation of Technological Universities, their predecessor Institutes of Technology, longer-established universities, national and sectoral documentation, student-union material and a Northern Ireland comparator strand.

The aim is not to treat document counts as evidence of stronger or weaker learner-centredness. The corpus is being developed as a structured evidential base for tracing how policy language, institutional priorities and organisational change are expressed over time.

Section 4 analytical workstream

The approved research design identifies three linked analytical outputs. They remain provisional and are being developed through literature and public documentary analysis before being tested and refined through later case-study research.

Enactment mechanisms framework

Develops explanations of the processes through which institutional conditions, interpretation and decision-making shape enactment.

Institutional conditions map

Tracks the governance, structural, cultural, regulatory and organisational conditions that shape what becomes possible.

Typology of enactment patterns

Develops patterns from documentary and later empirical evidence without assuming those patterns in advance.

Provisional analytical scaffold

The current working pathway is:

Policy commitment
→
Interpretation
→
Translation
→
Institutional decision / process
→
Enacted practice
→
Learner experience
→
Feedback / re-enactment

A central distinction under development is between conditions and mechanisms. Conditions shape what is possible; mechanisms concern the processes through which those conditions are interpreted and translated into institutional action.

Literature and documentary analysis

Builds the conceptual and policy basis of the study and maps publicly available evidence across Irish higher education.

Comparative qualitative case study

Planned for the empirical phase to investigate interpretation, discretion, institutional processes and lived experience in selected cases.

Practice-based documentary

Remains the preferred practice-based direction, but its final role and form will be determined by the research rather than fixed in advance.

Methodology, lens and position

Current methodological position

Qualitative research development

The present phase combines scoping review, desktop documentary research, analytical development and reflexive memoing.

Theoretical lens

Policy enactment

Used because the study examines interpretation, translation, negotiation and mediation rather than assuming linear policy implementation.

Research position

Interpretive-relational and insider-practitioner

Later empirical work will require explicit attention to role boundaries, power, reflexivity and institutional sensitivity.

Research development timeline

Year 1: literature, scoping review, public documentary corpus and analytical development. Year 2: strengthen and finalise the methodological design, develop ethics and access arrangements, and prepare the empirical phase. Year 3 onward: undertake comparative case-study work and develop the practice-based strand in response to the evidence generated.

This sequencing is intentional. The proposed documentary approach remains a strong direction, but the first year is also being used to test whether it remains the most defensible form for the eventual research output.

Ethical considerations

Ethics are central to the later participant and practice-based phases. Key considerations include informed and ongoing consent, confidentiality, appropriate anonymity or agreed attribution, data protection, role boundaries, power relations and layered consent for any material considered for multimodal or documentary representation.

The public-facing site does not publish formal ethics materials, participant data, internal institutional records or supervisory documentation.

Use of this site
