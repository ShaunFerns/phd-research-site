# Stage 3B.1 — Search concept map, working v0.6

Date: 31 October 2026. Status: exploratory, not an executable final search.

## Candidate concepts

A. Learner-centredness: learner-centred, learner-centered, learner-centredness, learner-centeredness, student-centred, student-centered, student-centredness, student-centeredness, and the corresponding learning phrases. Test hyphenation and indexing variants.

B. Higher education: higher education, universit*, tertiary education, postsecondary/post-secondary education, college*. The last term may be noisy in international searches.

C. Institutional relationship (exploratory, not necessarily mandatory): institutional change, governance, organisational/organizational conditions, policy enactment, institutional policy, implementation, curriculum reform, programme/program redesign, institutional culture.

Related terms for exploratory assessment, not assumed synonymous: student voice, learner agency, student partnership, co-creation, co-design, active learning, flexible learning, self-directed learning.

## Planned comparison

A sensitive baseline combines concept A AND concept B. A focused comparator adds concept C. Test both against approximately 8–12 independently verified PCC-eligible seed studies, selected across institutional settings and research approaches (including practice-based/led where available). Examine missed seeds, database controlled vocabulary and irrelevant retrieval; do not select solely by result counts. Translate to Scopus and ERIC-specific syntax only after exploratory analysis.

## Workflow

Export from databases; assess publication-date boundary in Zotero and verify ambiguous dates; deduplicate date-eligible records; then substantive screening. Keep source counts and date-removal counts.

## Methodological rationale

The substantive institutional relationship is an eligibility condition. A mandatory institutional terminology block may exclude relevant institutional research whose metadata lack those precise terms. The pilot should assess sensitivity and precision rather than optimise for a predetermined corpus size.
