PhD Research Overview

From Policy Commitment to Institutional Enactment

Learner-centredness in Irish higher education through policy and documentary analysis, comparative qualitative case study, and a later practice-based documentary strand.

Open presentation Read overview

Research focus

This PhD examines how learner-centredness is institutionally enacted in Irish higher education. The central concern is what happens between an institutional commitment to learner-centredness and its eventual expression in policy, governance, organisational processes and practice.

Current phase

The study is currently in its Year 1 framing phase. Work is focused on the literature review and desktop research using publicly available national, sectoral and institutional documentation. This material is being used to develop and test a provisional analytical scaffold and to identify questions that will require later empirical investigation.

Participant-facing research, privileged institutional material and other work requiring ethical review are planned for the later ethics and access phase.

Central research question

How is learner-centredness institutionally enacted in Irish higher education, and how do institutional processes align, translate or mediate national policy commitments?

Sub-questions

  1. How is learner-centredness constructed within national higher education policy and sectoral discourse in Ireland?
  2. How is learner-centredness interpreted and operationalised within selected institutional case sites?
  3. How do institutional actors describe the governance, interpretive and relational processes through which learner-centredness is enacted?
  4. What recurring cross-case patterns and tensions can be identified in the institutional enactment of learner-centredness?
  5. What enactment mechanisms and institutional conditions can be abstracted from the selected case sites?

Section 4 analytical workstream

The approved research design identifies three linked analytical outputs. They are being treated as a distinct workstream that can be developed provisionally through literature and public documentary analysis, then tested and refined through later case-study research.

Enactment mechanisms framework

Develops explanations of the processes through which institutional conditions, interpretation and decision-making shape enactment.

Institutional conditions map

Tracks the governance, structural, cultural, regulatory and organisational conditions that shape what becomes possible.

Typology of enactment patterns

Develops provisional patterns from documentary evidence that later empirical work can challenge, revise or replace.

Provisional analytical scaffold

The current working pathway is:

Policy commitment
→
Interpretation
→
Translation
→
Institutional decision / process
→
Enacted practice
→
Learner experience
→
Feedback / re-enactment

A central distinction under development is between conditions and mechanisms. Conditions shape what is possible; mechanisms concern the processes through which those conditions are translated into institutional action.

Literature and documentary analysis

Develops the conceptual and policy basis of the study and maps publicly available institutional documentation across Irish higher education.

Comparative qualitative case study

Will investigate interpretation, discretion, institutional processes and lived experience in selected institutional cases.

Analytic documentary

Will be developed later in response to substantive analytical and empirical findings, rather than being fixed in advance.

Methodology, lens and position

Current methodological position

Qualitative research development

The present phase combines literature, desktop documentary research, analytical development and reflexive memoing.

Theoretical lens

Policy enactment

Used because the study examines interpretation, translation, negotiation and mediation rather than linear implementation.

Research position

Interpretive-relational and insider-practitioner

Later empirical work will require explicit attention to role boundaries, power, reflexivity and institutional sensitivity.

How the strands connect

The Section 4 analytical outputs are intended to inform both the later interview design and the eventual practice-based documentary. The interviews can then investigate matters that documents cannot establish adequately, including interpretation, discretion, informal constraints and decision-making. The documentary can be developed from a substantive analytical base rather than serving as an illustration of findings already settled.

Ethical considerations

Ethics are central to the later participant and practice-based phases. Key considerations include informed and ongoing consent, confidentiality, appropriate anonymity or agreed attribution, data protection, role boundaries, power relations and layered consent for any material considered for multimodal or documentary representation.

The public-facing site does not publish formal ethics materials, participant data, institutional records or supervisory documentation.

Use of this site
